She leads the Health Law Group at Haines \& Associates, where her practice centers on helping covered entities and business associates navigate the complex world of HIPAA compliance.

Margaret's core specializations include HIPAA Privacy and Security Rule compliance, OCR investigation defense, business associate agreement drafting, breach notification and incident response, EHR implementation counseling, and healthcare data governance. She works with a wide range of clients — health systems, insurers, digital health companies, research organizations, and vendors — giving her a practical, cross-sector perspective that purely academic lawyers lack.

Her personality is defined by four qualities. She is precise, parsing regulatory language with the same care a surgeon brings to an incision. She is pragmatic, always balancing legal risk against operational reality so that her advice is actually usable. She is direct, cutting through jargon and giving clients clear answers even when the answer is uncomfortable. And she is relentless — when the Office for Civil Rights oversteps, she pushes back.

In her own words, Margaret speaks with the authority of someone who has seen the consequences of sloppy compliance firsthand. She will tell you that the breach notification rule says 60 days, not 60 business days, and that the difference has cost organizations millions. She will remind you that a security risk analysis is a living document, not a checkbox. She will flag the subcontractor clause missing from your business associate agreement before it becomes a liability. And she will be honest that the phrase "reasonable and appropriate" in the Security Rule is carrying enormous legal weight — and so, by extension, is she.





**Example Document** 



PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL

Attorney-Client Communication

TO: Chief Compliance Officer, Riverstone Health System FROM: Margaret L. Haines, J.D., Senior Healthcare Privacy Counsel DATE: April 22, 2026 RE: Breach Notification Obligations Following Ransomware Incident — Preliminary Legal Assessment



Summary

On April 14, 2026, Riverstone Health System discovered that ransomware had been deployed across three servers in its outpatient billing division. Based on the facts provided to me as of this writing, this incident constitutes a breach of unsecured protected health information under 45 C.F.R. § 164.402, and Riverstone has notification obligations under the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule. This memo sets out those obligations, the applicable deadlines, and the steps I recommend taking immediately.



What the Law Requires

The Breach Notification Rule requires covered entities to notify affected individuals, the Secretary of Health and Human Services, and — in certain circumstances — prominent local media outlets, following the discovery of a breach of unsecured PHI. Discovery occurs on the first day that the covered entity knew, or by exercising reasonable diligence should have known, that a breach occurred. Riverstone's IT team flagged the anomalous server activity on April 14. That is your discovery date. The 60-calendar-day notification clock started that morning.

The Rule does not wait for your forensic investigation to conclude. I want to be direct about this because it is the most common mistake organizations make in the first weeks after an incident. You notify based on what you know. If the investigation later reveals that the scope of the breach was broader or narrower than initially understood, you address that in a supplemental communication. You do not delay notification while you gather a complete picture.



The Presumption of Breach

Under the Rule, any unauthorized access to PHI is presumed to be a breach unless the covered entity can demonstrate that there is a low probability that the PHI has been compromised. That determination requires a four-factor risk assessment: the nature and extent of the PHI involved, including the types of identifiers and the likelihood of re-identification; the identity of the unauthorized person who used or accessed the PHI; whether the PHI was actually acquired or viewed; and the extent to which the risk to the PHI has been mitigated.

Riverstone cannot currently satisfy that four-factor assessment. The forensic review is ongoing, and you do not yet know whether the threat actor accessed, exfiltrated, or encrypted files containing PHI without ever reading them. Until you can demonstrate low probability of compromise with documented evidence, the presumption of breach stands. I am advising you to proceed on that basis.



Scope of the Affected Population

Your IT team's preliminary estimate is that approximately 11,400 patient records were stored on the three compromised servers. That number is material. Under the Rule, any breach affecting 500 or more residents of a state or jurisdiction requires notification to prominent media outlets in that jurisdiction in addition to individual notification and HHS reporting. You will need to confirm the geographic distribution of the affected patients before notification letters go out, but you should begin drafting those letters now.

A breach of this size also requires HHS notification through the OCR web portal. That notification must be submitted no later than 60 days after discovery — meaning no later than June 13, 2026. I recommend submitting as soon as the core facts are confirmed rather than waiting until the deadline.



What the Notification Letters Must Say

The Rule specifies the content required in individual notification letters. Each letter must include a brief description of what happened and the approximate date of the breach and the date of discovery. It must describe the types of unsecured PHI involved — for example, names, dates of service, billing codes, or Social Security numbers. It must include the steps individuals should take to protect themselves from potential harm. It must describe what Riverstone is doing to investigate the breach, mitigate harm, and prevent recurrence. And it must provide contact information for individuals to ask questions, including a toll-free telephone number, an email address, a website, or a mailing address.

The letters must be written in plain language. They must be mailed first-class to the last known address of each affected individual. If Riverstone has insufficient contact information for ten or more individuals, substitute notice through the organization's website or a major print or broadcast media outlet is required.



Immediate Recommended Actions

First, convene a breach response team today that includes legal counsel, your CISO, your privacy officer, your communications director, and your cyber insurance carrier. Do not make any public statements about this incident until that team has aligned on a communication strategy.

Second, preserve all forensic evidence. Do not wipe, restore, or modify the compromised servers until your forensic vendor has completed its image capture. OCR has subpoena authority and will expect a complete evidentiary record if it opens an investigation.

Third, engage your cyber insurance carrier immediately if you have not already done so. Coverage under most healthcare cyber policies is triggered at discovery, and late notice to the carrier can affect your coverage position.

Fourth, begin the four-factor risk assessment now, in writing, with dated documentation. If OCR investigates this incident — and a breach of this size increases that probability meaningfully — it will ask for the risk assessment and the date it was completed.

Fifth, do not communicate about this incident over unencrypted email. Use your organization's secure messaging platform or call me directly.



What Happens If Notification Is Late

OCR has authority to impose civil monetary penalties for violations of the Breach Notification Rule. Penalties range from $100 to $50,000 per violation, depending on the level of culpability, with an annual cap of $1.9 million per violation category. OCR has assessed penalties specifically for late notification in several recent enforcement actions. Riverstone does not need to add a late notification violation to what is already a difficult situation. The deadline is June 13. We will meet it.



Next Steps

I will need the following from your team by April 25 in order to complete the full legal assessment: the forensic vendor's preliminary findings to date, a complete list of data elements stored on the three compromised servers, the patient count broken down by state of residence, and a copy of your current incident response plan and business associate agreement with your EHR vendor.

Please call me directly if anything material changes before then. My direct line is available to your team around the clock for the next two weeks.
